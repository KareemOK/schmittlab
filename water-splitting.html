Water Splitting

Water splitting produces hydrogen (H₂) and oxygen (O₂) from water through two half-reactions: the hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) and the oxygen evolution reaction (OER). Driven by renewable electricity, water electrolysis offers a route to green hydrogen, a carbon-free fuel with the highest gravimetric energy density of any fuel. However, the leading electrocatalysts rely on scarce platinum-group metals such as platinum (Pt), iridium (Ir) and ruthenium (Ru), which are costly and sensitive to impurities in the water feed, limiting large-scale deployment.

Research Focus: Sustainable Electrocatalysts

The Schmitt group develops robust electrocatalysts based on earth-abundant metals as alternatives to noble-metal catalysts for both HER and OER. Our work centres on supramolecular materials, particularly metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) and coordination cages, whose structures allow catalytic activity to be tuned at the molecular level while remaining stable under demanding electrochemical conditions.
